San Francisco Got Let Off the Hook Under CJR. Under TEAM, It Became California’s Biggest Market.

Los Angeles stayed a mandatory market under an older Medicare bundled-payment program for nine straight years, specifically because it was expensive. San Francisco got downgraded to voluntary in 2018. Under TEAM’s own, separate lottery, LA drew exactly one hospital — voluntarily. San Francisco drew 37 — mandatorily, the most of any market in California.

Narration script

The three California markets, before and after

California had three markets in CJR’s original 67-MSA lottery: Los Angeles-Long Beach-Anaheim, San Francisco-Oakland-Hayward (renamed San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont under the 2023 OMB delineation), and Modesto. All three were mandatory when CJR launched in 2016. In 2018, CMS cut mandatory participation to the 34 MSAs with the highest historical episode spending — a cost-based cut, not a second lottery. LA’s spending was high enough to stay in. San Francisco’s and Modesto’s weren’t; both dropped to voluntary.

Six years later, TEAM ran its own, unrelated stratified draw across the whole country. The outcome for these same three markets:

Market CJR status (2018+) TEAM hospitals TEAM participation
Los Angeles-Long Beach-Anaheim Mandatory 1 (Cedars-Sinai) Voluntary
San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont Voluntary 37 Mandatory
Modesto Voluntary 0 Not selected

LA is the market this newsletter has already covered in depth: the country’s largest Medicare market, drawn out of TEAM’s mandatory lottery, with its one hospital — Cedars-Sinai — there entirely by its own election. What hadn’t been checked until now is what happened to the other two California markets that went through the same CJR history. The answer isn’t “the same bad luck twice.” San Francisco has more TEAM hospitals than any other market in California, mandatorily, and Modesto simply isn’t on the list.

Zooming out from three markets to sixty-seven

Three markets is an anecdote. The full CJR roster — all 67 MSAs from the original 2016 lottery — is a real sample. CMS no longer publishes the 2018 list of 34 mandatory MSAs as a single named table (its current CJR hospital list mixes mandatory and voluntary-continuing hospitals with no status column), so this analysis reconstructs it: re-applying CMS’s own stated 2018 criterion — highest average wage-adjusted historical episode payment — to the original 67-MSA roster, using CMS’s own 2015-rule payment data. The reconstruction reproduces all three known outcomes above as a check on itself before being used for anything else.

Against TEAM’s 188 selected CBSAs nationally (out of 803 total eligible CBSAs, metropolitan and micropolitan combined, under the current OMB delineation — a 23.4% baseline rate if the draw were blind to CJR history):

Group Selected into TEAM Rate
All 67 original CJR markets 19 of 67 28.4%
34 former-mandatory CJR markets 12 of 34 35.3%
33 former-voluntary-tier CJR markets 7 of 33 21.2%
National baseline (all eligible CBSAs) 188 of 803 23.4%

The corrected baseline changes the shape of the finding. Markets that went through CJR at all didn’t get a discount on TEAM’s lottery — if anything, they did slightly better than the national rate, 28.4% against a 23.4% baseline. And the split inside that group runs in one direction: the 34 markets CMS once forced into mandatory participation were selected at 35.3%, well above the national rate, while the 33 it let go voluntary landed close to it, at 21.2%. San Francisco isn’t an outlier inside this data — it’s sitting in the voluntary-tier group, which performed closest to what a blind national draw would predict. Los Angeles, in the higher-exposure mandatory tier, is the one whose individual outcome ran against its own group’s trend: that tier did better than average overall, and Los Angeles was still in the majority of it that didn’t land on TEAM’s roster.

What this doesn’t establish

TEAM’s selection, like CJR’s original lottery, is a stratified random draw — a result like this could reflect a real relationship between market characteristics and TEAM’s stratification variables (population, historical spending, something correlated with having been a large CJR market in the first place), or it could be within the ordinary noise of a draw this size. No significance test has been run on this, and none is claimed here. What can be said cleanly: in this one draw, the historical direction of “was CMS willing to force this market to participate before” ran toward more TEAM exposure, not less — the opposite of what a simple “same unlucky markets” story would predict, and the exact opposite of what happened to LA specifically inside its own market’s hospital count.

The 34-mandatory-MSA list itself is a reconstruction, not a CMS-published table copied verbatim — see “About the data” below before treating any single MSA’s status as certain.

What’s still unverified

Whether TEAM’s own stratification variables (the ones CMS used to draw its selected CBSAs) include anything that correlates mechanically with CJR history — population size and historical joint-replacement spending are plausible candidates, since both featured in CJR’s own stratification — hasn’t been checked. If TEAM’s strata happen to overlap with CJR’s, the pattern here could be a byproduct of both models drawing on similar underlying market characteristics rather than any information flow between the two programs. This would need TEAM’s own published stratification methodology cross-referenced against CJR’s, which hasn’t been done here.
