Narration script

Los Angeles has exactly one hospital in Medicare’s newest mandatory payment program, and so do Miami and Tampa. In all three cases, the explanation is the same: CMS’s lottery skipped the metro area entirely, and one hospital with nine years of history in a prior mandatory bundle used a narrow rule to volunteer in anyway, while every other hospital in a market of millions sat out.

Two more California markets show the identical outcome — one hospital, the entire TEAM footprint for that metro — and it is tempting to assume the same mechanism is at work. It isn’t. Sutter Coast Hospital, in Crescent City, and Adventist Health Hanford, in Hanford-Corcoran, are mandatory TEAM participants because CMS’s lottery landed directly on their metro areas, not because it missed them. And each is the only hospital on the roster there for the plainest possible reason: each is the only acute-care hospital in the market, period.

What TEAM asks of a hospital

The Transforming Episode Accountability Model is a five-year Medicare program that makes a hospital financially responsible for the cost and outcomes of a patient’s care for 30 days after five kinds of surgery: hip and knee replacement, spinal fusion, coronary artery bypass grafting, hip fracture repair, and major bowel surgery. 720 hospitals are on the roster. Selection isn’t by application — CMS divided the country into metropolitan and micropolitan areas, drew 188 of them by stratified random sample, and made every IPPS-paid acute-care hospital sitting inside a drawn area mandatory. A small number of hospitals outside the drawn areas can also join voluntarily, but only if they ran a mandatory CJR or BPCI Advanced bundle through its final months. That voluntary pathway is how Los Angeles, Miami, and Tampa each ended up with their one hospital.

Two markets, one hospital, same shape as LA — different cause

Sutter Coast Hospital (CCN 050417) sits in the Crescent City CBSA, CMS code 18860, one of the drawn areas. Adventist Health Hanford (CCN 050121) sits in the Hanford–Corcoran CBSA, CMS code 25260, also drawn. Sutter Coast Hospital is listed as a mandatory participant and Adventist Health Hanford as a mandatory participant — neither is a voluntary joiner, and neither is a late addition. CMS’s TEAM participant list marks both “N” for newly identified relative to the prior list, meaning neither is a recent addition; both have been on the roster since the program’s first performance year began January 1, 2026.

Each CBSA has exactly one hospital on the TEAM roster: 1 in Crescent City, 1 in Hanford–Corcoran. That much matches the shape of the LA/Miami/Tampa pattern. What doesn’t match is the reason. CMS’s Hospital General Information dataset — the same public registry that lists every Medicare-enrolled hospital in the country, filtered here to Del Norte County (where Crescent City sits) and Kings County (where Hanford, and its CBSA-mate Corcoran, sit) — shows exactly one acute-care hospital in each county, and it’s the same hospital in each case that’s already on the TEAM roster. Sutter Coast Hospital is the only Medicare-enrolled acute-care hospital anywhere in Del Norte County. Adventist Health Hanford is the only one in Kings County. Neither market has a second hospital that TEAM’s rule happened to catch or miss — there was never a second hospital to catch.

That also rules out the explanation this project flagged as worth checking first: a Critical Access Hospital quietly excluded from IPPS-based eligibility, leaving only one of several local hospitals mandatory by default. CMS’s own data classifies both Sutter Coast Hospital and Adventist Health Hanford as “Acute Care Hospitals,” not Critical Access Hospitals, and their CCNs (050417 and 050121) fall inside the 0001-0879 numbering block CMS reserves for short-term general and specialty hospitals — the same block as any other acute surgical hospital in the country, not the CAH-specific range. There is no excluded second hospital in either county to explain away. The one-hospital outcome here needs no exclusion mechanism at all; it’s just what the market looks like.

Neither hospital carries the CJR history that put Cedars-Sinai, Morton Plant, and the former Westchester General on the TEAM roster voluntarily. Checked directly against CMS’s own CJR hospital-list spreadsheets — the pre-February 2018 list and the July 2024 list, the model’s first and near-final rosters — neither CCN 050417 nor CCN 050121 appears on either one. Sutter Coast Hospital and Adventist Health Hanford are on TEAM because their metro areas were drawn, not because either was previously part of a mandatory bundle and chose to keep going.

The scale contrast is the opposite of LA’s

Los Angeles is the second-largest metro area in the country, with a Census-estimated population of roughly 12.9 million people; TEAM’s entire footprint there is one hospital that didn’t have to be there. Crescent City and Hanford-Corcoran run the comparison the other way. Del Norte County — the entirety of the Crescent City micropolitan area — had an estimated population of 27,009 as of July 1, 2024, according to the Census Bureau’s Vintage 2024 population estimates. Kings County, which is the entirety of the Hanford-Corcoran metropolitan area (Census classifies it as a full Metropolitan Statistical Area, not a micropolitan one, since its population clears the 50,000 threshold), had an estimated population of 154,913 the same year. Both are more than 80 times smaller than Los Angeles County’s metro population, and Crescent City’s is smaller than a single large hospital’s typical annual patient volume in a major city. TEAM’s rule doesn’t scale mandatory participation to market size — a hospital in a county of 27,000 people carries exactly the same one-hospital designation as a lottery-skipped hospital standing in for 12.9 million.

What isn’t established here

This piece checked CJR history for both hospitals against CMS’s pre-2018 and July 2024 hospital-list spreadsheets; it did not run an equivalent check against BPCI Advanced’s participant rosters, which CMS does not publish in the same consolidated spreadsheet format. Given that both hospitals are listed as Mandatory rather than Voluntary participants, a BPCI Advanced history would not change how either landed on the roster — but it’s worth flagging as unverified rather than assumed. Separately, this piece did not attempt to determine why CMS’s stratified sampling weights happened to draw these two specific small markets rather than other similarly sized ones; that would require the underlying sampling-weight methodology CMS has not published market-by-market. And the fresh CMS participant-list pull used to verify hospital-level detail here (dated August 18, 2026, 722 hospitals) carries two more hospitals nationally than the vendored copy this article’s 720 figure is computed from (dated April 15, 2026, 720 hospitals) — both list Crescent City and Hanford-Corcoran identically, but the discrepancy is noted rather than reconciled.
