Two California Markets Have Exactly One TEAM Hospital Each, Because That's All There Is.

Los Angeles has one hospital in Medicare's TEAM model because a huge metro got skipped by the lottery and one CJR veteran volunteered in. Crescent City and Hanford-Corcoran have one hospital each too, for the opposite reason: there's only one hospital in either market, and this time the lottery actually caught them.

Los Angeles has exactly one hospital in Medicare's newest mandatory payment program, and so do Miami and Tampa. In all three cases, the explanation is the same: CMS's lottery skipped the metro area entirely, and one hospital with nine years of history in a prior mandatory bundle used a narrow rule to volunteer in anyway, while every other hospital in a market of millions sat out.

Two more California markets show the identical outcome, one hospital, the entire TEAM footprint for that metro, and it is tempting to assume the same mechanism is at work. It isn't. Sutter Coast Hospital, in Crescent City, and Adventist Health Hanford, in Hanford-Corcoran, are mandatory TEAM participants because CMS's lottery landed directly on their metro areas, not because it missed them. And each is the only hospital on the roster there for the plainest possible reason: each is the only acute care hospital in the market, period.

What TEAM asks of a hospital.

The Transforming Episode Accountability Model is a five year Medicare program that makes a hospital financially responsible for the cost and outcomes of a patient's care for thirty days after five kinds of surgery: hip and knee replacement, spinal fusion, coronary artery bypass grafting, hip fracture repair, and major bowel surgery. Seven hundred twenty hospitals are on the roster. Selection isn't by application. CMS divided the country into metropolitan and micropolitan areas, drew a hundred eighty-eight of them by stratified random sample, and made every hospital paid under the inpatient system sitting inside a drawn area mandatory. A small number of hospitals outside the drawn areas can also join voluntarily, but only if they ran a mandatory CJR or BPCI Advanced bundle through its final months. That voluntary pathway is how Los Angeles, Miami, and Tampa each ended up with their one hospital.

Two markets, one hospital, same shape as LA, different cause.

Sutter Coast Hospital sits in the Crescent City metro area, one of the drawn areas. Adventist Health Hanford sits in the Hanford-Corcoran metro area, also drawn. Both are listed as mandatory participants, neither is a voluntary joiner, and neither is a late addition. CMS's TEAM participant list marks both as not newly identified relative to the prior list, meaning both have been on the roster since the program's first performance year began January first, twenty twenty-six.

Each metro area has exactly one hospital on the TEAM roster. That much matches the shape of the LA, Miami, and Tampa pattern. What doesn't match is the reason. CMS's own hospital directory, filtered here to Del Norte County, where Crescent City sits, and Kings County, where Hanford and its metro mate Corcoran sit, shows exactly one acute care hospital in each county, and it's the same hospital in each case that's already on the TEAM roster. Sutter Coast Hospital is the only Medicare enrolled acute care hospital anywhere in Del Norte County. Adventist Health Hanford is the only one in Kings County. Neither market has a second hospital that TEAM's rule happened to catch or miss. There was never a second hospital to catch.

That also rules out the explanation this research flagged as worth checking first: a critical access hospital quietly excluded from eligibility, leaving only one of several local hospitals mandatory by default. CMS's own data classifies both hospitals as acute care hospitals, not critical access hospitals, and their Medicare certification numbers fall inside the numbering block CMS reserves for short term general and specialty hospitals, the same block as any other acute surgical hospital in the country, not the critical access specific range. There is no excluded second hospital in either county to explain away. The one hospital outcome here needs no exclusion mechanism at all. It's just what the market looks like.

Neither hospital carries the CJR history that put Cedars-Sinai, Morton Plant, and the former Westchester General on the TEAM roster voluntarily. Checked directly against CMS's own CJR hospital list spreadsheets, the pre-February twenty eighteen list and the July twenty twenty-four list, the model's first and near final rosters, neither hospital appears on either one. Sutter Coast Hospital and Adventist Health Hanford are on TEAM because their metro areas were drawn, not because either was previously part of a mandatory bundle and chose to keep going.

The scale contrast is the opposite of LA's.

Los Angeles is the second largest metro area in the country, with a Census estimated population of roughly twelve point nine million people. TEAM's entire footprint there is one hospital that didn't have to be there. Crescent City and Hanford-Corcoran run the comparison the other way. Del Norte County, the entirety of the Crescent City area, had an estimated population of twenty-seven thousand as of mid twenty twenty-four. Kings County, the entirety of the Hanford-Corcoran area, had an estimated population of about a hundred fifty-five thousand the same year. Both are more than eighty times smaller than Los Angeles County's metro population, and Crescent City's is smaller than a single large hospital's typical annual patient volume in a major city. TEAM's rule doesn't scale mandatory participation to market size. A hospital in a county of twenty-seven thousand people carries exactly the same one hospital designation as a lottery skipped hospital standing in for twelve point nine million.

It's worth being honest about a few limits here too. This research checked CJR history for both hospitals against CMS's published hospital list spreadsheets, but did not run an equivalent check against BPCI Advanced's participant rosters, which CMS doesn't publish in the same consolidated format. Given that both hospitals are listed as mandatory rather than voluntary participants, a BPCI Advanced history wouldn't change how either landed on the roster, but it's worth flagging as unverified rather than assumed. Separately, this piece did not attempt to determine why CMS's stratified sampling weights happened to draw these two specific small markets rather than other similarly sized ones. That would require sampling weight methodology CMS hasn't published market by market.
