Narration script

Los Angeles has one hospital in Medicare’s TEAM model, and it’s there because it volunteered. San Francisco has 37, the most of any market in California, and every one of them is mandatory. Modesto has zero — not mandatory, not voluntary, none.

Los Angeles’ history is different from the other two. It was one of only 34 markets CMS kept mandatory in an older joint-replacement bundle, CJR, when the program narrowed its list in 2018 — staying in for all nine years of CJR’s run, 2016 through 2024. San Francisco and Modesto share a different history: both were downgraded out of mandatory status in that same 2018 cut, and both kept running CJR voluntarily through the program’s final day. Same drop, same continuation, the identical nine years in some form. One of them is now the largest TEAM market in California. The other has nothing.

What TEAM asks of a hospital

TEAM, the Transforming Episode Accountability Model, is a five-year Medicare program that makes a hospital financially responsible for the cost and outcomes of a patient’s care for 30 days after five kinds of surgery: lower-extremity joint replacement, spinal fusion, coronary artery bypass grafting, surgical hip and femur fracture treatment, and major bowel procedures. 720 hospitals are on the mandatory roster nationwide, selected not by application but by geography: any hospital paid under the Inpatient Prospective Payment System with a CMS Certification Number address inside one of 188 metropolitan or micropolitan areas CMS drew by stratified random sample, out of roughly 803 eligible areas, is in. Outside those 188 areas, a narrow voluntary pathway lets a small number of additional hospitals opt in anyway, if they ran a mandatory bundle of their own — CJR through December 31, 2024, or BPCI Advanced through December 31, 2025. 10 hospitals did, nationwide.

Three California markets ran CJR for nine years, in different mixes of mandatory and voluntary status. Their outcomes on TEAM’s separate, later lottery could not be more different.

Three markets, one history, three outcomes

California had three MSAs in CJR’s original 2016 mandatory lottery: Los Angeles-Long Beach-Anaheim, San Francisco-Oakland-Hayward (renamed San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont under the current OMB delineation), and Modesto. All three ran under mandatory CJR from 2016 through 2018. In 2018, CMS cut mandatory CJR participation to the 34 markets with the highest historical episode spending; Los Angeles’ spending was high enough to stay in, while San Francisco and Modesto dropped to CJR’s voluntary-continuation tier. All three, including the two that dropped tiers, kept running CJR episodes through the model’s final day, December 31, 2024 — a hospital only needed to have run the model at some point through that date to qualify later for TEAM’s voluntary opt-in, and CJR’s own hospital-list spreadsheets show hospitals in all three markets doing exactly that.

Then TEAM ran its own, unrelated stratified draw in 2024, and the three markets split three ways:

Market CJR status (2018 onward) TEAM hospitals TEAM participation
Los Angeles-Long Beach-Anaheim Mandatory 1 (Cedars-Sinai Medical Center) Voluntary
San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont Voluntary-continuation 37 Mandatory
Modesto Voluntary-continuation zero Not selected, no voluntary joiner

Los Angeles’ one hospital, Cedars-Sinai Medical Center (CCN 050625), is there because it used the voluntary opt-in built for hospitals with prior mandatory-bundle history. San Francisco’s 37 are there because CMS’s 2024 draw happened to select that CBSA outright, mandatorily, for reasons unrelated to its CJR history. Modesto’s CBSA wasn’t drawn, and unlike Los Angeles, no hospital inside it used the voluntary pathway to join anyway. Every plausible route onto TEAM’s roster came up empty for a market that ran the same nine-year CJR program as the other two, under the same voluntary-continuation status San Francisco also carried after 2018.

Confirming the zero

This finding rests on checking every row of CMS’s own roster, not just the mandatory ones. CMS’s TEAM Participant List — the same file this article’s other figures are computed from, and the same file CMS republishes quarterly — carries 720 hospitals total, mandatory and voluntary combined. None of them list a CBSA of Modesto, CA (CBSA code 33700). A fresh pull of that same file directly from CMS’s website, dated August 18, 2026 and carrying 722 hospitals (two more than the October 6, 2026 vendored copy this article otherwise uses, which is dated April 15, 2026 and carries 720), shows the identical result: zero Modesto rows, and the same one-hospital Los Angeles and 37-hospital San Francisco counts. The finding isn’t an artifact of a stale file.

It also isn’t an artifact of Modesto having no hospitals to send. CMS’s Hospital General Information file — pulled fresh from CMS’s provider-data API for this article — lists five acute-care hospitals in Stanislaus County, the single county that makes up the Modesto CBSA: Doctors Medical Center and Memorial Medical Center in Modesto, Stanislaus Surgical Hospital in Modesto, Emanuel Medical Center in Turlock, and Oak Valley Hospital District in Oakdale. None of the five appears anywhere on TEAM’s participant list, mandatory or voluntary.

The voluntary pathway specifically requires prior CJR or BPCI Advanced history through the program’s own final performance year. None of the five Stanislaus County hospitals shows up in CMS’s Model Year 7 BPCI Advanced participant list either — the roster covering the model’s last performance period, which ran through December 31, 2025. So the gap isn’t that a Modesto hospital was eligible to volunteer and chose not to. On the record CMS has published, no hospital in Modesto’s CBSA had a BPCI Advanced pathway available to it at all, and the one pathway that was available — CJR history — went unused.

Why markets with the same history landed differently

TEAM’s selection wasn’t blind to CJR history. CMS’s own final rule sorted every eligible CBSA into a stratum based on the median of four factors: average historical episode spending, the number of IPPS-paid acute-care hospitals in the area, the number of safety-net hospitals with qualifying episode volume, and — the factor that matters here — past exposure to CMS’s own bundled-payment models, including CJR and BPCI Advanced, between October 2013 and December 2022. CMS said explicitly, in the rule proposing this design, that it intended to oversample CBSAs with low past bundled-payment exposure, while still giving some high-exposure CBSAs a chance of selection rather than excluding them outright. A market with nine years of CJR participation — mandatory the whole way, like Los Angeles, or mandatory at first and voluntary after 2018, like San Francisco and Modesto — would plausibly land in the higher-exposure stratum CMS deliberately gave lower draw odds, not zero odds: San Francisco proves the higher-exposure stratum can still be drawn, but at lower odds than a market with no bundled-payment history at all.

That mechanism explains the general pattern already documented elsewhere in this research: former-mandatory CJR markets nationwide were selected into TEAM at 35.3%, well above the roughly 23.5 percent national baseline rate for all eligible CBSAs, metro and micro combined. Former-voluntary-tier markets — San Francisco and Modesto’s own group — landed close to that baseline, at 21.2%. It does not explain why San Francisco drew and Modesto didn’t, despite sharing the identical voluntary-tier history; that’s the ordinary variance of a probabilistic draw, and CMS has not published, and this article did not independently reconstruct, the exact selection probability assigned to Modesto’s specific stratum.

Size is the more concrete difference. Stanislaus County’s five acute-care hospitals are a small fraction of the hospital count in Los Angeles or San Francisco’s much larger CBSAs, and the number of IPPS-paid hospitals in a CBSA is itself one of CMS’s four stratification factors. Modesto’s metro population is about 557,000, roughly a fortieth of Los Angeles’ and a tenth of San Francisco’s-Oakland’s, and smaller than any of the three biggest one-hospital-TEAM markets this research has already covered elsewhere (Los Angeles, Miami, and Tampa, whose metro Medicare-enrollment footprints run from roughly 770,000 up to 2.3 million). A market this size sits far more plausibly in whatever “low” bucket several of TEAM’s four stratification factors define than a market the size of Los Angeles or San Francisco does — a plausible contributor to lower selection odds, though not one this article can isolate cleanly from the past-exposure factor above, since CMS classifies CBSAs on all four factors jointly rather than one at a time.

What isn’t established here

CMS has not published a hospital-by-hospital reconstruction of exactly which selection probability Modesto’s CBSA carried, so this article cannot say precisely how unlikely a zero outcome was, only that it fits a documented, deliberate oversampling design rather than an obvious error. The 2018 cut of CJR’s original 67 mandatory MSAs down to 34 is independently well documented for Los Angeles, San Francisco, and Modesto specifically, but the full 34-MSA list is this research bank’s own reconstruction, built by reapplying CMS’s stated criterion to CMS’s own historical payment data, not a verbatim CMS-published table — see this project’s prior published article on the broader 67-market pattern for that caveat in full. And nothing here should be read as CMS being unaware of, or having erred regarding, Modesto’s absence from TEAM; a stratified random draw is expected to produce outcomes like this one for some markets, by design.
